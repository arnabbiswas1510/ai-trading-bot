# Tune exits on the breakout population, not on the trades being eliminated

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Amends:** `2026-08-04_backtest-corrected-exit-parameters.md` (same day — its
  exit reversions were based on a biased sample and are themselves reverted here)

## Context

The previous ADR replayed the 13 real closed trades and, on that evidence,
reverted the widened profit ladder and the disabled time lever.

That was a **selection-bias error**, raised by the user: 11 of those 12 symbols
fail the new fundamental screener. Tuning exits on them optimises for a
population the bot is deliberately eliminating. The tight ladder "won" there only
because those stocks topped out at +4-9%; on stocks that actually run, a 2% trail
at +20% makes the right tail uncapturable.

The gap between the two populations is not marginal:

| | The 13 traded names | Real breakout entries in screener-passing names |
|---|---|---|
| n | 12 | 598 |
| Median 60-day MFE | +4.28% | **+18.33%** |
| Reaching +20% | **0%** | **46.3%** |
| Reaching +50% | 0% | 13.4% |

## Method

Entries are generated by the bot's own logic, replicating
`technical_screener.check_technical_breakout()`: close > SMA-50, volume >= 1.5x
the 50-day average, and close >= 95% of the 252-day high, with a 20-day cooldown
per symbol. Entry is at the next day's open. Exits are simulated on daily bars.

Everything is run over **two independent universes**:

1. **Screener-passing names** (79 symbols, 598 entries) — the population the bot
   will actually trade, but selected on *today's* fundamentals and therefore
   survivorship-biased.
2. **Broad unselected universe** (256 symbols, 2,314 entries) — price/volume/size
   filters only, no growth or sector screen, so it contains losers. Much weaker
   survivorship bias and far better statistical power.

A conclusion is only acted on if it holds in **both**. This matters because
survivorship bias specifically manufactures "wider stops are better", so that
finding needed the second universe to be trusted at all.

## Findings

### 1. The wide profit ladder wins on the real population

| Config | Broad (n=2,314) | Screener names (n=598) |
|---|---|---|
| Tight ladder + time lever (previous ADR) | +0.51% exp, 1.44 payoff | +0.11% exp |
| Wide ladder, time lever off | **+0.59% exp, 1.58 payoff** | **+0.18% exp** |

Consistent in both. The previous reversion is undone.

### 2. The Intraday Loss Minimiser is the most damaging exit in the system

| | Broad (n=2,314) | Screener names (n=598) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimiser on (2%) | +0.59% exp, 1.58 payoff | +0.18% exp, 1.30 payoff |
| **Minimiser off** | **+1.01% exp, 2.09 payoff** | **+0.60% exp, 1.80 payoff** |

Disabling it roughly doubles expectancy in both universes, and trades realising
+20% rise from 0.8% to 3.2%. This is the single largest exit effect found. It is
now **disabled by default** (`INTRADAY_MINIMISER_ENABLED=false`).

This is not a removal of downside protection: the IBKR trailing stop is
untouched. The minimiser was a second, much tighter exit layered on top of it,
and it was firing on developing winners.

### 3. The 7% base trailing stop is too tight — not acted on yet

| Base stop | Broad expectancy | Broad payoff |
|---|---|---|
| 7% (current) | +1.01% | 2.09 |
| 10% | +1.42% | **2.23** |
| 12% | +1.53% | 2.19 |
| 20% | +1.71% | 2.18 |

The effect is much larger in the survivorship-biased universe (+0.73% -> +4.97%)
than in the broad one (+1.01% -> +1.71%), which is exactly the signature of that
bias — so the true effect is nearer the smaller number. It is still real and
consistent, and payoff peaks around 10-12%.

**Deliberately not changed here.** Widening `STOP_LOSS_PCT` raises the maximum
loss on every position and is the one change in this analysis that materially
increases risk on live capital. It should be a conscious decision, not a side
effect of a backtest, and is left for the user.

### 4. The breakout timing signal has no measurable edge

Comparing breakout entries against random entries **in the same universe** (so
survivorship affects both arms equally):

| Hold | Breakout mean | Random mean | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 days | +1.04% | +1.06% | **-0.03%** |
| 22 days | +2.21% | +2.24% | **-0.03%** |
| 48 days | +5.25% | +6.65% | **-1.39%** |

Within a given universe, buying on a volume-confirmed 52-week-high breakout is
no better than buying on a random day, and worse if held longer. All of the
system's demonstrable edge comes from **which stocks are in the universe**, not
from when it buys them.

This is the most important finding for future work and it reframes the priority
order: entry *selection* > exits > entry *timing*.

## Decision

| Parameter | Before | Now | Holds in both universes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TRAIL_PROFIT_TIERS` | tight 2-5% ladder | **wide 5-6.5% from +20%** | Yes |
| `TRAIL_TIME_TIERS_ENABLED` | true | **false** | Yes |
| `INTRADAY_MINIMISER_ENABLED` | (always on) | **false** | Yes |
| `INTRADAY_PULLBACK_PCT` | 0.02 | 0.02 (unchanged; only applies if re-enabled) | — |
| `EARLY_LOSS_STOP_PCT` | 0.02 | 0.02 (unchanged) | — |
| `STOP_LOSS_PCT` | 0.07 | **0.07 — flagged, not changed** | Yes, but risk-increasing |
| Power-hold rule | kept, unvalidated | kept, now expected to bind (46% reach +20%) | — |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Exits are now tuned on the population the bot will actually trade, using
  entries generated by its own breakout logic rather than 13 hand-picked trades.
- Every acted-on change is confirmed on an unselected 2,314-entry universe, so it
  is not a survivorship artifact.
- The profit ladder and the power-hold rule are coherent again: the ladder no
  longer clamps to 2% at exactly the +20% point where power-hold engages.

**Negative / accepted risks**

- Removing the minimiser means positions that roll over will now run to the
  trailing stop instead of being cut early. Expect larger individual losses and
  longer holds; the backtest says the trade is strongly favourable, but it will
  look worse day to day.
- Daily bars cannot model intraday exits faithfully, so the minimiser's *cost* is
  measured on a daily proxy. Its direction is unambiguous and consistent across
  universes; the magnitude is approximate.
- The screener-passing universe uses current fundamentals (look-ahead). This is
  why no conclusion rests on it alone.
- Backtests assume unlimited slots and ignore the 4-position cap, ranking and AI
  scoring, so absolute expectancy figures are not portfolio returns.

## Follow-up

1. Decide on `STOP_LOSS_PCT` 7% -> 10% (finding 3). Risk-increasing; needs a
   human call.
2. Treat entry *timing* as the open problem (finding 4). The current trigger adds
   nothing over random entry — candidates: require the breakout to clear the
   pivot rather than sit within 5% of it, add a base-depth/tightness (VCP)
   condition, or demand relative-strength confirmation.
3. Re-run both backtests after any screener change, since exit tuning is
   population-dependent — that is the lesson of this ADR.
